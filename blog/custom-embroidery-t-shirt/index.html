Custom Embroidery T-Shirt

Jul 03, 2025

Approve a custom embroidery T-shirt as one system: the named knit garment, artwork at finished size, digitized stitch file, thread, needle, backing, hooping, placement and care route. First approve a sew-out on the actual fabric; then approve a finished pre-production shirt; finally convert that evidence into bulk first-off and line checks. A crisp digital logo or embroidery sample on stable cloth does not prove that the same stitches will sit flat, feel comfortable and survive care on a stretchy T-shirt.

Scope: this guide owns T-shirt-specific embroidery engineering from artwork audit through sew-out and finished-garment release. For complete supplier selection, commercial planning and shipment control, use the custom product manufacturer pillar guide .

This page answers one buying question: how should a supplier turn artwork into repeatable embroidery on a knitted T-shirt? It does not repeat the brand-application overview in custom embroidery branding solutions , the factory-continuity criteria in the custom T-shirt manufacturer guide , or campaign allocation in the bulk promotional T-shirt guide . Here, every decision ends in physical approval evidence.

State the wearer, channel, garment fit, use frequency, climate, care route, order quantity, size range, colors, logo positions and target delivery. A left-chest mark for office uniforms has different comfort and repeat-order needs from a large back emblem for an event. Identify which requirement leads: premium texture, small-run identity, wash durability or visual impact. Also state the target market because labeling, fiber claims and care information may affect the garment and packing specification. For cross-category brand architecture, see the custom caps and apparel brand program .

Freeze the T-shirt style, supplier article, fiber content, knit, nominal fabric mass, finish, colorway, rib, seam construction and size set before asking for a final embroidery file. A design digitized for a stable heavy jersey may pucker or sink into a lighter, more elastic knit. If stock garments and custom-cut garments are both under consideration, treat them as different platforms. Retain a labeled sample and lot reference so the approved sew-out can be traced to bulk.

Fiber and nominal GSM are useful identifiers, not complete behavior. Record whether the body is single jersey, interlock, pique or another structure; note elastane, surface finish, usable width and recovery. Ask for fabric direction and face. Compare color lots because dyeing and finishing can change hand or dimensional behavior. The technical file should name the actual article, not merely “100% cotton T-shirt,” which leaves the supplier free to substitute a visibly similar but less stable base.

Single jersey can curl and distort more readily around concentrated stitches, while pique has a more textured surface that may affect edge clarity and coverage. Neither is automatically better. Run the same finished-size design on each proposed base with its intended backing, then compare fabric flatness, detail, reverse feel and recovery after care.

Measure or at least compare stretch and recovery in the two main garment directions under an agreed method. Map the result to the logo orientation and hooping plan. Excess movement while sewing can create registration gaps, outlines that miss their fills and rippled fabric after the hoop is released. Avoid solving instability only by adding dense stitches or excessive backing; that may create a stiff plaque. The approval target is a balanced assembly that returns flat without sacrificing coverage.

If you are briefing a run, send quantity, material direction and artwork. Tendlyn Studio replies from Guangzhou with a sample plan.
