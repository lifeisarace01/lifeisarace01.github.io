How Much Do Custom Bags Cost? 2026 Pricing Guide

  • Product Introduction

Posted by Trimela On Jul 23 2026

How much do custom bags cost? For early budgeting, a simple non-woven shopper or polyester drawstring bag may sit below USD 2 per piece at a workable production quantity, while a lined canvas tote, cooler bag or multi-compartment backpack may range from several dollars to USD 20 or more. Those figures are not quotations. They are broad ex-factory planning bands, and the final price depends on the exact product, order quantity, materials, branding, packaging and trade terms.

A useful price therefore starts with a useful specification. Two bags can look similar in a photograph but carry different fabric weights, reinforcements, zippers, linings and inspection requirements. If you are still deciding which format fits the project, begin with our product range. For the wider sourcing framework, return to the Custom Bag Manufacturer Guide.

Bag type is only the starting point. Fabric, components, decoration and construction determine what the factory is actually pricing.

Typical custom bag cost ranges by type

The table below gives indicative USD ex-factory unit ranges for an OEM order using generally available materials and ordinary branding at approximately 500–1,000 pieces per style or color. It excludes samples, tooling, inspection, freight, duties, tax and special testing. A small stock-based run may price differently, while a fully custom design can move outside the range.

Bag type Indicative unit range Typical basis What can raise the price

Non-woven shopping bag USD 0.40–1.50 Standard fabric, simple seams, one print position Higher GSM, lamination, gussets, full-surface print

Polyester drawstring bag USD 0.70–2.50 Stock polyester, standard cord and corners Lining, pockets, reinforced channels, custom fittings

Cotton tote bag USD 1.20–4.00 Standard cotton weight, flat construction, screen print Heavier fabric, custom dye, zipper, pocket, certified material

Heavy canvas tote USD 2.50–8.00 Structured canvas, gusset and reinforced handles Lining, metal hardware, embroidery, complex internal layout

Cosmetic pouch USD 1.50–6.00 Common zipper, simple lining and compact shape Special surface, shaped panels, branded puller, binding

Cooler bag USD 3.00–12.00 Standard insulation, zipper and carry handles Thicker foam, leak-resistant lining, structure, pockets

Backpack USD 5.00–25.00+ Basic daypack with standard webbing and zipper Laptop protection, multiple compartments, molded parts, testing

Travel or duffel bag USD 8.00–35.00+ Medium-size bag with lining and standard hardware Large volume, reinforced base, premium fittings, wheels

Planning note: these bands are deliberately broad and are not standing offers. Currency, material markets, factory loading and specification changes can move a quotation. Compare written quotes only when they use the same dimensions, material, construction, decoration, quantity, packing and Incoterm.

The eight factors that change a custom bag price

1. Bag type, dimensions and capacity

A flat tote needs fewer pattern pieces and operations than a padded backpack. Size also changes material consumption, cutting layout, packed volume and carton count. Give finished dimensions and the intended load rather than describing the bag only as small, medium or large.

2. Main material and fabric weight

Material is often the largest visible cost driver. Cotton, canvas, non-woven polypropylene, standard polyester, recycled fabrics, coated textiles and synthetic leather have different purchase units, usable widths and cutting yields. Fabric weight matters too: a 12 oz canvas tote is not the same product as a light cotton giveaway bag.

3. Construction and sewing time

Every gusset, pocket, binding, reinforcement, curved seam and panel adds work. The cost is not simply the number of parts; it is also how easily the parts can be cut, aligned and sewn consistently. A design that requires several machine changes or manual finishing will normally cost more than a clean, repeatable construction.

4. Lining, padding and structure

Lining adds material and another sewing layer. Foam, board, quilting and reinforcement increase both protection and production steps. They may also change carton volume. Buyers should specify where structure is required instead of asking for the entire bag to feel “premium.”

5. Zippers, buckles and other hardware

Standard stock components keep sourcing straightforward. Custom-colored zippers, plated metalwork, molded pullers and branded buckles can introduce tooling charges or supplier minimums. Ask the manufacturer to identify which component creates the longest lead time or highest minimum.

6. Logo method, size and positions

A one-color screen print on one flat panel is a different quote from front-and-back printing, dense embroidery, a woven patch or an all-over sublimated design. Decoration cost depends on setup as well as running time. Confirm artwork size, color count, location and whether the bag must be decorated before or after sewing.

7. Quantity, colors and SKU split

Fixed work—pattern preparation, sample development, color approval and print setup—is spread across the order. A 2,000-piece order divided into four colors may behave like four smaller production runs. The typical MOQ for custom bags explains why the total quantity is not the only number that matters.

8. Packing, testing and delivery terms

Individual polybags, tissue, hangtags, printed boxes, barcode labels and set assembly all add material or labor. Testing, third-party inspection and special documentation should appear in the budget as well. Finally, EXW, FOB, CIF and DDP quotations cover different portions of the journey; a low EXW number cannot be compared directly with a delivered price.

Cost driver Lower-cost direction Higher-cost direction Best question to ask

Material Stock color and standard weight Custom dye, coating or certified specialty fabric What equivalent stock material is available?

Construction Flat panels and standard seams Many pockets, binding, padding and shaped pieces Which feature adds the most sewing time?

Branding One position with production-ready art Several locations, dense embroidery or custom badge Can the same visual goal use a simpler method?

Components Stock zipper, webbing and buckle Custom plating, color or mold Which component has a separate MOQ or tooling fee?

Packing Standard bulk or simple individual packing Retail box, inserts, labels and kit assembly What is included in the quoted packing?

A quotation becomes more reliable when the buyer and factory review the same physical details—not only a reference photograph.

Unit price is only one line in the budget

Buyers often focus on the number multiplied by quantity. That matters, but development and delivery costs can change the commercial result, especially for a first order. Separate recurring unit costs from one-time or project-level costs so repeat orders are easier to forecast.

Cost layer Examples Usually recurring? How to document it

Development Pattern, prototype, courier and revisions Mostly first order or major redesign Sample quotation with revision limits

Tooling and setup Print screens, embroidery digitizing, molds or fixtures May be reusable if artwork and supplier stay the same Separate line item and ownership or storage terms

Production Materials, cutting, decoration, sewing and packing Yes Unit price tied to the approved specification

Quality assurance Testing, inspection and corrective action Depends on buyer program Inspection scope, test standard and responsibility

Logistics Export handling, freight, insurance, duty and local delivery Yes, but changes by route and timing Incoterm, destination, carton data and validity date

Useful formula: landed cost per usable bag = (production + setup + sampling + inspection + freight + duty/tax + local handling) ÷ accepted saleable quantity. This exposes costs that disappear when a comparison looks only at the factory unit price.

Why the cheapest unit price can become the most expensive option

A unit price is easy to place in a spreadsheet, but it does not show the full cost of owning and using the bags. A cheaper bag can create more defects, product damage, repacking, complaints, urgent replacements or shorter reuse life. Those costs appear after the purchase order, which is why they are often missed during supplier comparison.

For reusable packaging and branded bags, calculate both landed cost per accepted bag and, when relevant, cost per use:

  • Landed cost per accepted bag = product, development, tooling, inspection, freight, duties and handling, divided by the number of bags that pass acceptance.

  • Cost per use = landed cost per accepted bag divided by the realistic number of uses before failure or replacement.

For example, a USD 2.00 tote that is used twice costs USD 1.00 per use. A USD 3.00 tote that performs reliably for ten uses costs USD 0.30 per use. The higher unit price produces the lower lifecycle cost—provided the use assumptions are realistic and the product specification supports the expected durability.

Also record internal operating costs. Extra sorting, relabeling, repacking, complaint handling and emergency air freight can erase a small unit-price saving quickly. When quality affects a product launch or customer experience, include the commercial value of delivery stability and brand presentation in the decision.

How quantity changes the economics

Unit price normally improves as fixed setup is spread over more pieces and material purchasing becomes more efficient. The movement is not perfectly linear. A price break may appear when an order uses a full roll, a better cutting marker, a complete decoration batch or a stable sewing run. Ask for price tiers built around the factory’s actual production logic.

Quantity position Likely unit-price pattern Commercial advantage Main risk

Below normal MOQ Highest; may include a surcharge Tests demand with less inventory Setup dominates and choices may be limited

At workable MOQ Balanced starting point More stable materials and production Forecast still needs validation

Next efficient tier Often meaningfully lower Better use of setup and material More cash and storage committed

Large repeat program Best negotiated structure when demand is proven Scheduling and repeatability improve Forecast, warehousing and version control matter more

Transparent custom bag pricing: what every quotation should show

A responsible factory cannot give one permanent price for every canvas tote or custom bag. Cotton prices, fabric weight, dyeing, decoration, hardware, packaging, quantity and trade terms change the cost. “Fixed low prices” are risky when the supplier has not confirmed the specification because the missing detail may later become a surcharge or an unapproved substitution.

Quotation line What must be stated Low-price warning sign Buyer verification

Fabric Fiber, weave, weight or thickness, color and finish “Canvas” without confirming 8 oz, 10 oz or 12 oz Approve a labeled swatch and finished sample

Construction Dimensions, seams, handles, reinforcement, lining and pockets Reference photo used instead of written specifications Use a measurement chart and bill of materials

Branding Method, colors, size, position, coverage and setup Printing fee appears only after artwork approval Request the setup and per-piece charge separately

Development Pattern, sample, revisions, tooling and courier Unexpected pattern or film charges after deposit Confirm inclusions and revision limits in writing

Packing and inspection Individual or bulk packing, labels, cartons and QC scope Packing, testing or inspection excluded silently Approve a packing specification and inspection plan

Commercial basis Quantity split, MOQ, Incoterm, payment, validity and schedule EXW, FOB and DDP prices compared as if identical Normalize every offer to the same scope

Ask every shortlisted supplier to sign off on the same specification and quotation comparison sheet. Then use our custom bag manufacturer checklist to compare quality control, communication and production capability alongside price.

How to compare quotations fairly

A quotation is only comparable when its assumptions are visible. One supplier may use 10 oz canvas and standard bulk packing; another may use 12 oz canvas, a lining and individual bags. The second quote is not automatically expensive—it may simply describe a different product.

Quote field What must match Common hidden difference

Product specification Dimensions, fabric, weight, lining and pockets One supplier quietly substitutes a lighter fabric

Decoration Method, artwork size, colors and positions Setup is excluded or only one location is included

Quantity split Total, colors, artworks and permitted over/under run A color minimum appears after the order is placed

Packing Individual packing, labels, carton quantity and protection Retail presentation is treated as an extra later

Quality basis Approved sample, tolerances, tests and inspection plan The quote has no measurable acceptance standard

Trade term EXW, FOB, CIF or DDP plus named location Two unit prices cover different delivery responsibilities

Before comparing numbers, understand the custom bag manufacturing process. Then use the questions in How to Choose a Custom Bag Manufacturer to assess whether the supplier can explain its assumptions and control the finished result.

Seven ways to reduce cost without making the bag feel cheap

  • Protect the bag’s job. Keep the load, capacity and user experience clear; simplify details that do not support them.

  • Use an existing construction as a starting point. Change the dimensions or features that matter instead of rebuilding every pattern piece.

  • Choose stock fabric and trim. A close available color can avoid custom dye and component minimums.

  • Reduce colorways. Put more volume behind fewer production lots, especially on the first order.

  • Match decoration to the artwork. Do not pay for dense embroidery when a well-designed print or woven label achieves the intended look.

  • Standardize hidden components. Use proven zippers, webbing and internal materials where they do not change the customer-facing promise.

  • Separate launch packaging from permanent packaging. Begin with practical packing, then add complex retail presentation once demand is proven.

Cost control works best during design, not after a finished specification has been quoted. For more detail, read How to Reduce Cost When Sourcing Custom Bags in Bulk.

Information a factory needs for an accurate quote

  • Bag type, intended use and target load

  • Finished dimensions and reference photos or drawings

  • Main fabric, approximate weight and color

  • Lining, padding, reinforcement and internal organization

  • Zipper, buckle, webbing, handle and hardware requirements

  • Logo artwork, method, size, colors and positions

  • Quantity by color and artwork

  • Individual packing, labels and carton needs

  • Testing, compliance or inspection requirements

  • Delivery destination, target date and requested trade term

When the specification is still flexible, say which details are fixed and where the manufacturer may propose alternatives. A clear brief creates room for useful value engineering instead of an artificially low price based on assumptions.

Frequently asked questions

How much do 500 custom bags cost?

The answer can range from a few hundred dollars for a very simple stock-style bag and logo to several thousand dollars for lined canvas totes, cooler bags or backpacks. Multiply the confirmed unit price by 500, then add sampling, setup, inspection and logistics. The specification and permitted color split matter more than the round quantity alone.

Does a logo cost extra?

Often, yes. A quote may include one standard print, but additional colors, positions, embroidery, patches or labels can add setup and unit costs. Ask exactly what artwork size, method and number of locations are included.

Are samples free?

Existing samples may be free apart from freight, while a made-to-spec prototype normally carries a development charge. Some suppliers credit part of the sample fee after a bulk order, but the written terms should state this clearly.

Is shipping included in the unit price?

Only if the quotation says so. Confirm the Incoterm and named location. An FOB price covers a different portion of the supply chain from EXW or DDP, so the headline unit numbers cannot be compared without that context.

Why did the price change after sampling?

The sample may reveal material, construction or decoration details that were not included in the initial estimate. Ask for a revised bill of materials and a list of specification changes so the reason is visible.

Why can’t a factory give one fixed price for every custom canvas bag?

Because the price depends on fabric weight and composition, dimensions, construction, logo method, quantity, packaging and trade terms. A fixed figure given before these details are confirmed is only an assumption. Ask the supplier to state the specification and exclusions behind the number.

How do I calculate the true cost per accepted bag?

Add unit cost, development, tooling, inspection, packaging, freight, duties and handling. Add an allowance for defects or replacements, then divide by the number of bags that pass acceptance. This produces a more useful comparison than the quoted unit price alone.

What should a transparent custom bag quotation include?

It should identify the material and weight, bag dimensions and construction, branding method, quantity and color split, sample and tooling fees, packaging, testing, inspection, Incoterm, payment terms, schedule, quote validity and every exclusion.

Turn a price question into a workable quotation

Custom Bag Pricing becomes useful when the number is attached to a measurable product. Define the bag’s purpose, dimensions, material, construction, logo, quantity split, packing and delivery basis. Then compare the full landed cost and the supplier’s ability to repeat the approved sample—not only the smallest unit price.
