How Much Do Custom Shopping Bags Cost? 2026 Pricing Guide

  • Product Introduction

Posted by Trimela On Aug 27 2026

A custom shopping bag can cost less than a cup of coffee or more than the product placed inside it. That is not a pricing contradiction. “Shopping bag” covers paper carriers converted at high speed, sewn jute bags, laminated woven-PP grocery shoppers, clear TPU presentation bags and many constructions in between. Each has its own material yield, print setup, labor and freight profile.

This Shopping Bag Pricing Guide explains how to build a useful 2026 budget without treating a search-result price as a factory quotation. It focuses on the economics of retail carriers: size families, handles, gussets, artwork splits, folding and cost per delivered thousand. For broader bag categories, see How Much Do Custom Bags Cost?. If you are comparing suppliers rather than budgets, use How to Choose a Custom Shopping Bag Manufacturer and the Custom Bag Manufacturer Guide.

Trimela’s custom shopping bag range includes paper, reusable, jute, mesh, canvas and transparent directions. That breadth matters because the lowest-cost construction changes with the checkout load, presentation level, reuse target and order quantity.

A meaningful price begins with the physical cost stack: body material, handles, decoration, converting, quality control and pack-out.

Important: the ranges below are 2026 planning bands for the reference specifications shown, usually on an EXW basis. They exclude freight, duties, taxes and destination charges. They are not an offer from Trimela or a promise that every supplier can meet the same specification. Size, test requirements, artwork, packing, order timing and destination can move the final price materially.

2026 custom shopping bag price ranges: the short answer

For an order of roughly 1,000–5,000 units per design, a basic printed non-woven shopper may sit near the lower end of the market, while structured jute, heavy canvas or clear presentation bags occupy higher bands. A premium paper carrier can be cost-efficient at volume, yet lamination, rigid handles and special finishes quickly change its position.

Shopping bag route Reference specification for planning Illustrative EXW band, 1,000–5,000 pcs What commonly moves it

Non-woven PP Stock-color 80 gsm body, medium size, one-color print, basic handle US$0.30–0.80 each Sewn vs ultrasonic construction, fabric weight, gussets and print area

Laminated woven PP Medium grocery shopper, printed film laminate, bound edges and webbing handles US$0.70–1.80 each Film structure, full-coverage graphics, binding, reinforcement and bag geometry

Paper carrier 250 gsm coated paper, matte lamination, rope/cotton handles, standard retail size US$0.38–0.70 each Paper grade, dimensions, handle, print coverage, foil and other finishing

Jute shopping bag Natural jute, gusset, simple print, basic cotton/webbing handles US$0.90–2.50 each Jute grade, lamination, lining, edge finish, handle and sewing detail

Cotton or canvas shopper Gusseted sewn bag, midweight fabric, one-color print US$0.90–3.00 each Fabric weight, fiber specification, dyeing, pockets, zipper and reinforcement

Clear PVC or TPU retail bag Simple clear body, textile binding/trim, zipper or handled opening US$1.20–3.50 each Film grade/thickness, welding, trim complexity, protection and compliance testing

These bands deliberately stay broad. Public supplier references illustrate why a specification is essential: one published custom paper-bag table prices 250 gsm coated paper with matte lamination and cotton handles by size and quantity, while a non-woven sourcing reference lists 80, 100 and 120 gsm options with a 1,000-piece minimum. The figures are not interchangeable because the products are not identical.

What is actually inside the unit price?

A factory’s unit price is the result of six layers: materials, conversion labor/machine time, decoration, setup/tooling allocation, quality and packing, plus commercial margin. The final Custom Shopping Bag Cost is therefore a construction result, not a price attached to a product photo. The percentage carried by each layer changes by route. Paper cost is heavily influenced by sheet area and yield. In a jute bag, sewing and trimming have greater weight. A laminated woven-PP bag adds printed film and lamination before assembly even begins.

Route Largest variable-cost pool Typical setup cost Hidden cost buyers miss

Paper Paper area, coating/lamination and handle system Plates, die and finishing setup Large gussets reduce sheet yield; foil or spot effects add passes

Non-woven PP Spunbond weight plus bonding/sewing route Screen/plate and machine setup A low body price can omit strong handle patches or wide seams

Laminated woven PP Woven substrate, printed film and lamination Cylinder/plate, artwork separation and lamination setup More artwork SKUs fragment film runs and increase changeovers

Jute/canvas Material grade, cut yield and sewing minutes Screen, pattern and jig Natural variation, fray control and bulky packing

Clear film Film, binding/zipper and welding or sewing labor Cutting die/template, print and welding setup Scratch protection and individual packing can be substantial

This is why a credible quotation describes the construction, not just a picture. It should state finished dimensions, body grade or weight, handle material and size, reinforcement, print method and coverage, packing and trade basis. A price that omits those details is difficult to compare and easy to change later.

Price driver 1: size, gusset and material yield

Small dimensional changes are not always small cost changes. Adding 20 mm to a gusset may push a paper layout onto a larger sheet or reduce the number of panels cut across a fabric width. Increasing handle drop adds material to every unit. A bottom board creates another component and may make the folded bag thicker.

Start with what the store actually sells. Make a “normal basket” and a “maximum sensible basket,” then prototype around them. An oversized bag wastes body material and shipping cube on every order. An undersized one produces double-bagging at checkout, which destroys the apparent saving.

For multi-size programs, look for shared handles, paper grade, fabric color and print ink. Common components do not remove each SKU’s setup, but they can simplify purchasing and reduce leftover material.

Price driver 2: body material and construction route

Material should be quoted as a measurable specification. “Kraft paper,” “non-woven,” “jute” or “canvas” is not enough. Record paper basis weight and finish; polypropylene gsm and whether it is virgin, recycled or laminated; jute construction and lamination; fabric composition and weight; or transparent-film type and thickness.

For paper claims, the FSC chain-of-custody system traces eligible forest-based material through the supply chain. For recycled-content claims, the current Global Recycled Standard has scope and transaction requirements. Certification, testing and documentation can add cost, but an unsupported claim creates a different and potentially larger business risk.

Do not select by gsm alone. A heavier body with weak handles is not a stronger shopping bag. The relevant question is how the entire load path—base, gussets, seams/bonds, reinforcement and handles—behaves with the intended products.

Price driver 3: printing and brand finish

Decoration cost is governed by setup, number of passes, coverage, substrate behavior and reject risk. A small one-color screen print on stock non-woven is economical for a simple logo. Photographic artwork on a woven grocery bag is usually more logical as printed film before lamination. Premium paper may justify offset printing, foil or embossing, but each finish needs its own setup and quality control.

Brand treatment Best-fit shopping bag routes Cost behavior Approval risk

One-color screen/flexo print Non-woven, jute, canvas and simple paper Low unit cost after setup; each color/design adds setup Ink holdout, opacity and texture can change appearance

Multi-color offset print Premium paper carriers Efficient at volume; plates, make-ready and finishing remain Color shifts after lamination; artwork at folds and glue laps

Printed film + lamination Woven PP and selected non-woven bags Strong full-coverage value at scale; higher setup and route minimum Registration around gussets and laminate bond

Heat transfer/digital route Shorter runs or complex textile graphics Lower setup in some cases, higher unit application cost Hand feel, edge, wash/rub behavior and color consistency

Foil, emboss/deboss or spot finish Premium paper and selected synthetic surfaces Tooling plus an additional production pass and yield risk Fine detail, pressure, alignment and cracking near folds

Approve the effect on the actual substrate. A screen preview cannot predict ink absorption on jute, film gloss after lamination or metallic-foil behavior across a paper crease.

Price driver 4: handles, reinforcement and retail function

Handles are both a design feature and a structural system. Twisted paper, flat paper, cotton rope, ribbon, die-cut openings, non-woven loops and sewn webbing have different material and attachment costs. A long shoulder drop consumes more material and creates greater leverage on the bag wall.

Ask the supplier to specify attachment: glue patch, folded turn-in, ultrasonic bond, box stitch, cross stitch, rivet or another method. Then define a load test based on the shopping basket. Paying for the correct reinforcement is usually cheaper than using a heavier body to compensate for an under-engineered handle.

Price driver 5: MOQ, SKU count and artwork splits

Factories quote by runnable quantity, not by the headline purchase order total. An order for 6,000 pieces divided among three sizes, two colors and two logos can behave like twelve 500-piece jobs. Material may be shared, but printing setup, cutting, line change, sorting and carton identification repeat.

6,000-piece order design Runnable quantity Cost effect Smarter option

One size, one color, one artwork 1 × 6,000 Best dilution of setup and simplest packing Use when the product basket and store format are consistent

Three sizes, same material/color/artwork 3 × 2,000 Separate patterns/dies and line setup; some inputs can pool Standardize handles, substrate and print color

Two colors × three artworks 6 × 1,000 More material segregation and repeated print changeovers Keep body color common and vary a label, belly band or one print plate

Three sizes × two colors × two artworks 12 × 500 May fall below route minimums; highest setup and sorting load Launch a controlled core range, then add proven variants on reorder

Low MOQ is therefore not automatically low commitment. A tiny unit count can require expensive digital decoration, stock materials, surcharges or a much higher price per bag. For the broader logic, see What Is the Typical MOQ for Custom Bags?

Three realistic budget patterns

1. Boutique paper carrier

A fashion or beauty retailer may prioritize crisp color, a comfortable rope handle and a clean fold. The body material is only part of the budget; lamination, handle fitting, die cutting and finish control carry weight. The best saving is often a disciplined size family and one shared paper/handle specification, not stripping away the presentation that customers notice.

2. Reusable grocery program

For woven or non-woven PP, look beyond first cost to service use. A stronger handle, base reinforcement or easier-cleaning surface can raise unit price while reducing replacement and improving perceived value. Divide delivered cost by the intended credible uses—but do not advertise an unverified reuse number.

3. Seasonal premium shopping bag

A holiday jute, canvas or clear bag may combine special material, multiple trims and gift-ready packing. The risk is not simply “expensive material”; it is late approval, fragmented art variants and rework on visible details. Lock the hero treatment early and standardize the invisible construction wherever possible.

Price driver 6: packing density and freight cube

Shopping bags often occupy freight by volume before they reach a weight limit. Paper gussets, rope handles, base boards, jute texture and sewn seams all create folded thickness. A unit price that saves three cents but adds a bulky handle arrangement can cost more per delivered thousand.

A physical pack-out trial converts folded thickness into carton count, CBM and handling—not just an estimated packing line in a spreadsheet.

Pack-out measure How to verify it Why it affects cost Decision it supports

Conditioned thickness of 50/100 bags Pack production-representative units and measure after a defined hold Predicts units per bundle/carton more reliably than one loose bag Fold, handle lay and compression method

Units per master carton Count a physical trial carton and reopen sample units Changes carton material, handling and total shipment count Protection vs packing density

External carton dimensions Measure the closed packed carton, not an empty box Determines cubic volume and dimensional calculations Freight plan and pallet/container fit

Gross carton weight Weigh the sealed sample carton Affects handling limits and weight-rated transport Carton count, material and safe lift

Delivered condition Open units after a representative packed hold or transit trial Over-compression can deform paper, crack finishes or mark clear film Interleaving, bagging and compression limit

Ask for carton dimensions, gross weight, units per carton and total CBM with the quote. When the bag changes, repeat the pack-out. Do not reuse an old carton assumption for a new handle or thicker body.

EXW, FOB and landed cost are not comparable prices

A factory number may stop at the workshop, at an export handover point or at a named destination. The ICC Incoterms® 2020 rules allocate specific costs, risks and obligations between seller and buyer. They do not define the bag, inspection level, payment terms or every destination charge.

Quote basis Confirm in writing Add before comparison Common mistake

EXW named place Exact pickup point, packing and readiness Origin pickup/handling, export tasks, main freight, insurance if selected, import and inland delivery Comparing it directly with a delivered number

FOB named port Named port, included origin charges and document scope Main freight, insurance if selected, import and destination/inland charges Assuming all pre-shipment inspection or certificates are included

CIF named destination port Named port, freight basis and insurance scope Import, destination terminal/handling as applicable and inland delivery Reading “port price” as warehouse-delivered cost

Delivered/landed model Named address, service scope, exclusions and tax/duty assumptions Any explicitly excluded tax, brokerage, accessorial or storage exposure Using a generic duty rate without product/destination classification

Normalize every supplier to one commercial basis: same quantity per SKU, specification, quality/test scope, packing, named place, currency and validity date. Then compare total cash, arrival timing and risk—not unit price alone. Buyers importing from China can use How to Import Custom Bags from China as a broader workflow.

How to reduce cost without weakening the bag

  • Design around the shopping basket. Remove unused volume before reducing material performance.

  • Standardize the hidden system. Share body color, handles, reinforcement and cartons across visible artwork variants.

  • Put complexity where customers notice it. One strong brand surface often performs better than several small premium processes.

  • Protect runnable quantity. Consolidate artwork and avoid splitting the order into uneconomic micro-SKUs.

  • Engineer the fold. Reduce empty cube without crushing handles, creases or clear surfaces.

  • Approve once, clearly. A frozen sample, artwork and bill of materials prevent change and rework.

  • Plan the reorder. Retain color, print, component and carton standards so the second run does not restart development.

Never remove a reinforcement or lower a material grade only because it is invisible. Test a value-engineered sample with real products, then accept the change only when appearance and function remain suitable.

Frequently asked questions

How much is one custom shopping bag?

At roughly 1,000–5,000 units per design, planning may start near US$0.30 for a simple non-woven route and extend above US$3 for structured canvas or clear specialty bags. The reference specification matters more than the headline range. Request a live quote for the exact size, material, decoration, packing and delivery basis.

Why do two shopping bags that look similar have different prices?

Photos hide paper weight, fabric grade, film structure, handle length, reinforcement, seam/bond method, print coverage, inspection and packing. Those differences affect performance and production cost even when the outside shape looks similar.

Does a larger quantity always lower the price?

Usually it spreads setup and improves material/run efficiency, but only within a stable specification. If volume is split across many sizes, colors and artworks, each variant may remain a short run. Storage, obsolescence and cash tied in unused bags can also outweigh the unit saving.

Are paper shopping bags cheaper than reusable bags?

Sometimes, but not automatically. A simple paper carrier at scale may cost less than a sewn reusable bag. Premium coated paper with rope handles, foil and rigid reinforcement can exceed a basic non-woven option. Compare a defined product and, where relevant, a credible cost per use.

How much does custom printing add?

It depends on the method, colors, coverage, order split and substrate. One-color printing is often economical after setup. Full-coverage laminated graphics and specialty finishes carry more setup and process steps. Ask for unprinted, standard-print and premium-finish alternatives on the same specification.

Are tooling and sample charges included?

Not necessarily. Confirm plates/screens/cylinders, dies, pre-production samples, courier, testing and repeat-order ownership separately. A low unit price can exclude one-time costs that materially change the project total.

What is the cheapest way to lower shopping bag cost?

Reduce unused size, standardize components, consolidate variants, simplify decoration and improve packing density. Validate any material or reinforcement reduction with a representative sample and load test.

How should I compare three supplier quotes?

Put them on one line-by-line specification and one commercial basis. Compare quantity per SKU, body material, handles, construction, printing, setup, sample/testing, quality, packing, carton data, trade term, named place and validity. Treat missing detail as an unresolved cost or risk.

Price the retail system, not the product photo

A useful Custom Shopping Bag Price answers three questions: what exactly will be made, how will it perform at checkout and what will it cost at the agreed handover point. The lowest factory unit is not always the lowest delivered or per-use cost. Size discipline, runnable SKU quantities and physical pack-out can matter as much as the material name.
