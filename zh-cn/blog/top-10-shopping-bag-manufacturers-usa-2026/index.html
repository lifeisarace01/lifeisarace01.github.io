What “Made in USA” establishes—and what buyers must still check

The FTC explains that an unqualified US-origin claim requires US final assembly or final processing, all significant processing in the United States, and all or virtually all domestic components and ingredients. Qualified claims can describe a more limited US contribution when they are truthful and supported.

A US warehouse or local printing operation alone is insufficient evidence for an unqualified claim. See the FTC’s Made in USA guidance.

Ask the supplier for the wording it can substantiate on the quoted item. Keep three questions separate in the approval record: where the bag is made, where its important inputs originate, and what wording may appear on the product or marketing page.

Supplier statement What it tells the buyer Next verification step

“Made in USA” without qualification A broad origin claim Request substantiation supporting that wording

US checkout rules can change the material decision

California illustrates why retail location belongs in the RFQ. From January 1, 2026, covered stores generally may provide only compliant recycled-paper carryout bags at the point of sale, subject to the law’s definitions and exclusions; customers may bring reusable bags. This is not a universal prohibition on every plastic bag in every setting. The paper requirement moves to at least 50% postconsumer recycled material in 2028. Check the current CalRecycle bag requirements for the intended store and use.

For a multi-state program, have the buyer’s compliance team approve a destination matrix before purchasing. Record store category, bag function, required materials and markings, fee treatment where relevant, and the date of review. Reconfirm the applicable state and local requirements before rollout. This guide provides sourcing context rather than a nationwide legal determination.

No. Suitability depends on the store, destination rules and product function as well as performance. A bag sold as merchandise or brought by a customer can be treated differently from one provided at checkout. Confirm the intended use before selecting material.

Is buying from a US company faster than importing?

It can be, but the useful comparison is the actual order schedule. Confirm stock availability, artwork approval, materials, production queue, shipping and receiving requirements. A supplier’s location alone does not establish the delivery date.

Can I buy directly from every manufacturer on the list?

Buying channels vary. Some accept project inquiries, some use distributors or resale accounts, and some offer catalog ordering.Confirm the commercial route before spending time on a detailed quotation request.

What minimum order should a retailer expect?

Minimums vary with conversion, material, dimensions, decoration and release structure. Request the minimum per design and color, then distinguish that from the quantity released to each store. A published minimum is a dated starting point for discussion.

What should be approved before the first production run?

Approve a written specification, physical sample where appropriate, artwork, origin and claim wording, inspection requirements, case pack and delivery responsibilities. Identify who can approve substitutions and how a material or plant change will be reported.

Send the bag’s role, destination states and store type, size, contents/load, material preference, artwork, quantity by design, packing instructions and required arrival date. Include origin restrictions and any claim or testing requirements at the start.
