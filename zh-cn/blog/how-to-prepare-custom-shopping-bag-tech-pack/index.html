how to prepare a 定制购物袋 tech pack

  • 产品介绍

发布 Tendlyn Studio 于 2026年8月28日

how to prepare a 定制购物袋 tech pack。广州 Tendlyn Studio 按项目打样与大货。

A shopping bag tech pack should tell four different teams the same story. Costing needs measurable consumption and process assumptions. The sample room needs geometry, materials and a decision list. Production needs construction and artwork instructions. Quality and logistics need test methods, SKU identity, folding and carton data. If any team has to reconstruct the product from emails, the pack is incomplete.

This guide explains How to Prepare a Custom Shopping Bag Tech Pack for paper, non-woven PP, laminated woven PP, jute, cotton/canvas, mesh and clear-film retail carriers. It does not repeat the all-category Custom Bag Tech Pack guide or the compartment-heavy Cosmetic Bag Tech Pack guide. The focus here is the retail operating system: shopping basket, checkout opening, gussets, handles, converted artwork, store allocation and flat-pack logistics.

Review possible directions on LUCKYSTAR’s custom shopping bag product page. If supplier selection is still open, use How to Choose a Custom Shopping Bag Manufacturer and the wider Custom Bag Manufacturer Guide.

A factory-ready pack connects the assembled carrier to every physical panel, fold, handle and reinforcement that creates it.

Quick answer: build one controlled file with a retail-use brief, route decision, dimensioned views, points of measure, material/component BOM, construction sections, artwork map, SKU matrix, functional tests, packaging and revision log. Use drawings for instructions, physical samples for feel/color, and data for tolerances and acceptance.

What belongs in a shopping bag tech pack?

Do not begin with a 30-page template. Begin with the decisions the assigned factory must make. A straightforward stock-color non-woven shopper may need a concise pack. A full-print woven-PP grocery bag with several store artworks needs film maps, gusset registration, handle/reinforcement and allocation pages.

Packing and allocation Fold, bundles, protection, cartons, labels, store/warehouse quantities and CBM data Packing/logistics How usable goods arrive

1. Establish document control before adding drawings

Assign one style code and one pack owner. Put the revision on every exported page and attachment, not only the cover. A filename such as “final-final2” cannot tell a cutting room whether the handle update belongs with the latest artwork.

Keep three statuses separate: for costing, for sample and released for bulk. Early costing may contain visible assumptions. Sample versions convert assumptions into testable choices. Bulk release should contain no silent open point; any unavoidable sample-to-bulk difference needs an owner and first-off approval gate.

Maintain a short change log: version, page/component changed, reason, old instruction, new instruction, approver and effect on price/MOQ/timing. The related Custom Shopping Bag MOQ and Production Time guide explains why apparently small material or artwork changes can reopen an upstream batch or schedule.

2. Put the retail job on page one

The factory does not need a marketing persona before it knows what the bag carries. List the normal basket, maximum sensible basket and awkward product. State whether the carrier is free packaging, a sold reusable item or a promotional giveaway. Describe how staff take, open, fill and store it.

Retail brief field Minimum useful entry Tech-pack consequence Evidence during development

Normal basket Named items, dimensions, mass and loading order Usable W × H × gusset and mouth Filled-bag photos/observations

Maximum sensible basket Largest/heaviest intended single-bag combination Body, base, handle and reinforcement design Documented load-path trial

Checkout station Counter width, staff motion, storage and expected opening behavior Fold direction, top stiffness and handle lay Representative checkout trial

Carry context Hand/shoulder use, clothing, walking duration and comfort priority Handle drop, width/diameter and edge feel Carrying review with named load

Brand view How graphics should read flat, filled and carried Artwork orientation and converted safe area Flat and filled approval photos

Store/logistics model SKU quantities, bag storage, units/carton and destinations Colorway matrix, labels, fold and carton plan Pack-out and allocation reconciliation

Give the largest real product or an accurate dummy to the sample review. Exterior dimensions alone do not reveal whether a rigid box catches on a gusset or a bottle makes the base unstable.

3. Select the production route before finalizing the drawing set

“Shopping bag drawing” means different things to different factories. A paper carrier needs a converting dieline with glue lap, turns, folds, handle holes/patches and bottom sequence. A direct-print non-woven bag needs cut or roll-fed geometry and bond/seam widths. Laminated woven PP needs the printed film layout connected to the converted gusset. Jute/canvas needs grain/warp-aware patterns and seam allowances. Clear film needs cut and joining sections.

Clear PVC/TPU Cut film, opening, trim/binding and handle/zipper views Weld/seam width, overlap, protective film and trim interface Film face, joining method and scratch-protection stage

Use solid lines for cut edges, a consistent convention for folds and clear sections for hidden construction. Include a datum for dimensions and print registration. The factory may refine the final dieline or marker for its machinery; the buyer should approve the changed geometry before artwork or material consumption is frozen.

4. Build a shopping-bag points-of-measure chart

A sketch with W × H × D is not enough. State whether measurements are taken flat, lightly opened or filled; whether gusset depth is fold-to-fold; and whether handle drop is measured vertically from the top edge to the inside of the handle.

Assign tolerances by function and process instead of copying one value across every POM. Paper, bonded PP, sewn natural fiber and flexible clear film have different controllability. Confirm the manufacturer’s proposed method during sampling, then freeze it with the approved reference.

5. Write the BOM as a route-specific recipe

The bill of materials should identify every input that changes performance, appearance, compliance, cost or packing. “Blue fabric, rope handle” is not a specification. Add supplier/internal code, composition/grade, weight or thickness, color/physical standard, finish, placement, source status and approval reference.

Packing material Interleaf/bag, bundle closure, master carton, label and pallet protection Physical pack-out trial Count, identity, carton dimensions and condition

If the project specifies responsibly sourced paper, recycled PP or recycled textile content, keep the product claim, approved material and supply-chain evidence aligned. FSC describes chain of custody for eligible forest-based material. Textile Exchange published GRS version 5.0 on July 1, 2026. Do not paste a certification logo into a tech pack as a substitute for scope and order-level verification.

6. Draw construction sections that reveal the load path

Show what a front view hides. For a rope-handle paper bag, section the top turn, cord end, reinforcement patch and panel. For ultrasonic non-woven, section the bond width and intersection. For a sewn gusset, show seam allowance, turn direction, binding and reinforcement. For clear film, show overlap and joining method.

Every product load travels from the base/body through seams, bonds or folds into the handle attachment. Mark that system, not just a body gsm. Specify whether reinforcement sits inside or between layers, how far it extends, and what operation secures it.

Use a callout identifier that links drawing, BOM line and quality checkpoint. If the supplier proposes an alternate construction, add it as a new revision rather than overwriting the original intent in a chat.

7. Map artwork on the converted bag

Artwork must be placed on the final route-specific dieline or pattern. Indicate front, back, both gussets, base, glue lap, binding, top turn, seams and handle zones. Set the print direction and the visual datum for placement.

A continuous graphic should be approved on both the flat print layout and the opened converted gusset; the two views answer different questions.

If a retail barcode is required, follow the applicable GS1 specification for that symbol and market. GS1 notes that barcodes need sufficient contrast and clear quiet zones; its 2D retail guidance also warns against interference from graphics, folds or other packaging elements. The tech pack should identify the data owner and verification responsibility rather than inventing a code inside the artwork file.

8. Define quality as methods and defect decisions

“Good quality” cannot guide an inspector. Define the reference, method and decision. For dimensions, name the POM and conditioning. For load, name the filler, mass, distribution, lift/cycle or duration and failure rule. For print, name color/placement reference, lighting and adhesion/rub method as appropriate. For packing, name fold, count and carton evidence.

ISO’s current ISO 2859-1:2026 provides AQL-indexed lot-by-lot sampling schemes for inspection by attributes. A sampling plan does not create product requirements. The tech pack or purchase quality agreement still needs to classify critical, major and minor defects and state methods, limits and rejection/corrective-action rules.

9. Treat packing and store allocation as product specifications

A rope handle can emboss a laminated paper panel; jute can transfer lint; clear film can scratch; thick seams can increase folded cube. Specify the fold diagram, handle position, interleaving or protection, units per bundle/carton and acceptable compression. Require the packed-carton dimensions and gross weight to come from a physical trial.

Separate product SKU from destination allocation. Identical bags sent to 50 stores remain one production SKU but require controlled carton labels and quantities. If body color, size, handle or artwork changes, create a distinct SKU and map it to the correct inner/master label.

10. Connect the tech pack to sampling and bulk

The tech pack is not completed once and filed away. During development, every approved change should update its source page. The related Custom Shopping Bag Sample Development Process shows how dimension dummies, material/handle kits, print proofs, checkout trials and production-reference samples close different decisions.

Before bulk, reconcile four items: signed sample, pack revision, supplier BOM/material orders and production work instructions. If they disagree, stop and resolve the source. After bulk, retain the inspection, packing and shipment records with the same style/revision for repeat-order comparison.

Common failure: the sample is approved after a handle or material change, but the tech pack and purchase order still describe the earlier version. The factory then has two legitimate-looking standards. Approval is not closed until physical and written references agree.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a complete tech pack before contacting a manufacturer?

No. A clear retail brief, dimensions or reference, material direction, artwork, quantity split and destination are enough for an initial feasibility review. Mark unknowns. The supplier can help finalize route-specific dielines and production details, but the approved revision must eventually capture them.

How many pages should a shopping bag tech pack contain?

As many as needed to remove risky interpretation—often roughly 8–20 for a focused style, plus artwork and allocation attachments. A complex full-print multi-SKU program may require more. Page count matters less than revision control and usable detail.

Can I use a photo or existing shopping bag as the specification?

Use it as a reference, not the complete instruction. Measure the desired outcome, identify which features to copy or change, and create route-specific drawings, BOM, artwork, tests and packing. Photos hide material grade, reinforcements, glue/seams and tolerances.

Should paper and reusable shopping bags use the same template?

They can share document control and retail-use sections, but the technical pages must differ. Paper needs converting folds/glue/handle patches; bonded PP needs bond geometry; laminated PP needs film maps; jute/canvas needs patterns and seams; clear film needs joining and protection.

Who should create the final dieline?

The buyer supplies design intent and required finished geometry. The assigned manufacturer or converter should normally issue or validate the production dieline for its equipment. The buyer then approves the geometry before final artwork is released.

How should tolerances be selected?

Choose them by function, route and process capability. Tighten dimensions that control product fit, handles or artwork only where necessary. Agree the measurement method during sampling; a tolerance without a datum and conditioning method is ambiguous.

Is an approved sample enough for bulk production?

No. The sample shows the accepted physical outcome. Bulk teams also need POM/tolerances, BOM, artwork/color reference, construction, test methods, SKU allocation and packing. Reconcile the sample and written pack before release.

What files should accompany the tech pack?

Include editable and reviewable drawings, print-ready vector artwork, color references, SKU matrix, sample/revision log, test requirements and packing/label files. Use controlled filenames and list every attachment on the cover or document register.

Build a specification that works at checkout and on the line

A useful Shopping Bag Specification Sheet is not a decorative design deck. It translates the retail job into route-specific geometry, components, converted artwork, functional methods and packing. The final test is simple: can a costing engineer, sample maker, line supervisor and inspector reach the same product without guessing?
