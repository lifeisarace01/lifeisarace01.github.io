定制化妆包 sample development process

  • 产品介绍

发布 Tendlyn Studio 于 2026年8月23日

定制化妆包 sample development process。广州 Tendlyn Studio 按项目打样与大货。

A cosmetic bag sample can look attractive on a desk and still fail when a buyer places a tall bottle through the opening, loads the brush panel or closes the zipper around a full kit. Sampling is not a beauty contest. It is the controlled process of turning a brand idea into a physical reference that can be measured, used, corrected and reproduced.

The custom cosmetic bag sample development process therefore needs category-specific decisions. Outer dimensions matter, but so do usable internal volume, lining behavior, zipper path, corner bulk, pocket access, surface cleanability, branding on the actual material and the way the finished bag fits its retail packaging.

This guide concentrates on those cosmetic-bag decisions. For the cross-category sampling framework, use the Custom Bag Sample Development Process. For factory capability, communication and evidence, read How to Choose a Cosmetic Bag Manufacturer. The wider sourcing structure sits in the Custom Bag Manufacturer Guide, while current formats can be explored through LUCKYSTAR’s custom cosmetic bags.

Quick answer: what is the cosmetic bag sampling sequence?

A disciplined route normally moves through six decision layers: define the use and specification; approve material and trim direction; build a functional first prototype; review the bag with real contents; correct the construction and branding; then approve a pre-production sample linked to the written specification. A shipment reference or retained production sample may follow when the program needs it.

The names used by factories vary. “Counter sample,” “development sample,” “approval sample,” “salesman sample” and “PP sample” are not universal standards. Define what each sample must prove instead of relying on the label alone.

Stage Physical output Main decision Do not approve yet

  1. Brief and feasibility Annotated drawing, reference review or volume mock-up Use, size, construction route, target cost and open questions Bulk material, final color or workmanship

  2. Materials and components Labeled outer, lining, foam, zipper, hardware and logo trials Material family, finish, compatibility and sourcing route Finished bag performance from loose swatches alone

3.First functional prototype Complete sewn or welded bag, sometimes using substitute colors Proportion, opening, capacity, pockets, lining and assembly logic Final appearance when approved materials were unavailable

  1. Revised approval sample Corrected bag plus resolved logo and component choices Comments closed and intended use validated Mass production if bulk inputs or packing remain different

  2. Pre-production sample Bag made to the frozen production specification Reference for bulk materials, dimensions, construction, branding and packing Unrecorded verbal changes after sign-off

6.Production or shipment reference Retained sample selected from the approved production route Comparison with bulk output and future repeat orders Assuming one unit proves every unit is conforming

1. Choose the development route before requesting a sample

Not every project should begin with a completely new pattern. A promotional pouch may use an available construction with custom color and logo. A retail beauty accessory may need modified dimensions, lining and hardware. A signature vanity case may require full pattern development. The route changes sample cost, number of decisions, tooling exposure and time to approval.

Development route What is already controlled Typical sample focus Best fit

Private-label or available style Pattern, basic structure and known production method Color, logo, trim and packaging application Fast promotions and lower-development programs

Adapted construction A proven base pattern and assembly route Changed dimensions, pockets, lining,opening or handle Brands needing useful differentiation without a new platform

Full custom OEM Buyer brief, tech pack or physical reference Pattern, proportion, engineering and every visible component Original retail products with defined requirements

ODM development Use case, brand direction and commercial constraints Supplier-proposed shapes, materials and feature options Teams needing design-to-manufacturing support

Use How to Design a Cosmetic Bag Collection when the project includes several coordinated sizes or styles. Sampling every idea at once is expensive; identify the lead construction and shared components first.

2. Prepare a sample brief that removes avoidable guessing

A reference photo shows visual direction, not a complete product.The sample maker still needs to know what the bag carries, how it opens, whether it must stand, what the inside should do and how it will be sold. Mark which features are fixed, which are targets and where the manufacturer may recommend an alternative.

The brief can be concise, but it must be controlled. If drawings are available, connect every view to a revision number and date. The How to Prepare a Custom Bag Tech Pack guide explains drawings, measurement points, materials, construction and artwork in more detail.

Brief item What to provide Why the sample maker needs it

Program and user Retail SKU, GWP, travel kit, subscription box, salon or another use Sets durability, presentation and budget priorities

Actual contents Dimensions, weights and arrangement of bottles, jars, brushes or samples Defines capacity, opening and internal organization

Finished measurements Width, height, depth, opening,handle and critical pocket sizes Creates a measurable pattern target

Layer schedule Outer, backing, foam/interlining, lining and placement Controls hand feel, shape and sewing bulk

Components Zipper, puller, binding, elastic, mesh, handle and hardware Confirms compatibility and sourcing assumptions

Branding Vector artwork, dimensions, colors, method, position and safe area Allows an actual-size logo trial

Packing and quality Fold/shape, tags, insert, retail box, tests, tolerances and defect concerns Prevents the sample from being approved outside its delivery context

3.Approve materials and trims as traceable components

A loose swatch should have a supplier reference, composition, weight or thickness, color reference and intended layer. A green outer fabric and green lining can look similar in a photograph while behaving very differently in sewing, cleaning and color transfer. Keep the physical cutting and its written identification together.

For a cosmetic bag, review the outer and lining as a system. The lining may need enough ease to turn and close without pulling, but too much slack can catch in the zipper or feel unfinished. Foam and backing may improve shape while adding corner bulk. The material guide, Best Materials for Custom Cosmetic Bags, helps match the layer schedule to the product’s use.

Component approval Check on the loose sample Recheck on the finished prototype

Outer material Color, grain, weight/thickness, backing and surface Corner behavior, seam marks, wrinkles and finished hand feel

Lining Color, coating, cleanability and print Slack, pocket access, zipper interference and staining

Interlining or foam Thickness, recovery and bonding Shape, edge bulk,creasing and carton recovery

Zipper system Chain, tape, slider, puller, color and finish Curve, end stops, access and operation under load

Mesh, elastic and binding Width, stretch, shade and edge Product retention, comfort, alignment and seam bulk

Metal or molded hardware Dimensions, finish and attachment route Noise, pull force, rotation, scratches and surrounding reinforcement

4. Build the first prototype to answer functional questions

The first prototype should not be expected to prove everything. If final fabric is unavailable, a substitute may be useful for proportion and construction, provided the differences are listed. If the final zipper pull is still in tooling, a standard puller can test the opening. Separating functional and appearance questions prevents a useful prototype from being rejected for details it was never meant to represent.

Before the sample ships, ask the manufacturer to photograph it beside a ruler and show the outside, inside, base, opening, pockets, logo and packing. This does not replace physical review, but it can catch obvious misunderstandings before courier cost and review time are spent.

First-sample question Evidence Possible correction

Does the silhouette match the intended position? Front, side and loaded comparison with the drawing Pattern proportion, gusset, radius or reinforcement

Can the user reach the contents? Largest item through the opening and removal test Longer zipper, wider mouth or changed internal divider

Does the bag stand or fold as intended? Empty, partially loaded and fully loaded views Interlining, base geometry,piping or layer weight

Does the lining behave correctly? Inside-out review, pocket access and repeated closing Lining pattern, attachment, ease or binding method

Are stress points supported? Handle, wrist strap, divider and pocket attachment review Reinforcement, seam allowance or attachment position

5. Conduct a loaded-fit review with the real beauty products

External dimensions do not equal usable capacity. The zipper tape, corner radius, lining, padding and dividers consume space. A bottle may fit inside yet fail to pass through the opening. Brushes may fit when laid flat but bend when the lid closes. Approve the sample with the intended contents in their realistic arrangement.

Loaded-fit check How to review it Failure the empty sample can hide

Entry clearance Insert and remove the largest item without forcing the zipper Adequate volume but an opening that is too narrow

Closing clearance Close slowly while observing contents, lining and zipper path Bottle cap or brush pushes into the chain

Pocket retention Turn,carry and gently shake the loaded bag Elastic or mesh allows products to escape

Weight distribution Lift by the intended handle or wrist strap Bag twists, tips or strains an attachment

Partial-load behavior Repeat with fewer products Structure collapses or products move excessively

Pack-out fit Place the loaded or empty bag into the intended insert, sleeve or box Corners crush, logo is hidden or package will not close

If the requirement involves liquid exposure, distinguish wipe-clean, splash-resistant and a defined water test. A visually convincing sample is not automatically waterproof. Review the construction principles in Waterproof Cosmetic Bag Manufacturing.

6.Approve logo methods through strike-offs before the full sample

A digital mock-up can confirm approximate scale and placement, but not opacity, texture, edge quality, heat response, puckering or color on the chosen material. A strike-off is a small production trial using the intended process and substrate. It can often resolve logo risk before a full revised bag is sewn.

Logo route Strike-off review Finished-bag review

Screen printing Color, opacity, edges, hand feel and rub behavior Position from finished seams and distortion on the loaded bag

Heat or digital transfer Detail, color, film edge, adhesion and surface change Panel shape, crease zones and proximity to sewing

Embroidery Thread colors, density, detail and backing Panel puckering,lining coverage and balance on the bag

Debossing or foil Depth, sharpness, foil transfer and surface recovery Final placement and visibility on curved or padded panels

Label, patch or metal plate Size, color, finish and attachment concept Alignment, reinforcement, scratches and user contact

7. Turn sample comments into one controlled revision list

“Make it more premium” is not an actionable sample comment. State the location, observed condition, required result, measurement or reference and whether the change is mandatory. Mark photos directly and give each issue a number. Combine design, merchandising, quality and packaging comments into one approved file before sending them to the supplier.

Every new sample should arrive with a change log: what was requested, what was completed, what remains open and what could not be followed. Review the unchanged areas as well. Correcting the zipper opening must not silently change the finished height, lining or logo placement.

8. Separate design approval from quality acceptance

A sample approval confirms a defined reference; it does not prove that every bulk unit will be identical. Hand-made samples can receive individual attention, and some materials may come from small sample quantities rather than the booked production lot. Bulk acceptance still needs tolerances, defect rules and inspection.

Record which aspects are approved exactly, which have a tolerance and which are visual references. A color swatch may govern the outer shade, a measurement chart may govern size, a logo strike-off may govern artwork and the PP sample may govern overall assembly. The Cosmetic Bag Quality Control Checklist explains how these references support incoming, in-line and final checks.

Color and finish Approved physical reference under defined viewing conditions Lot comparison and agreed tolerance

Logo Actual-size strike-off and placement drawing First-piece and in-line placement/appearance review

Packing Packing specification and approved pack trial SKU, label, quantity and carton verification

9.Approve the pre-production sample as a package of evidence

The pre-production sample should reflect the frozen production route as closely as practical: intended materials, color, zipper, hardware, branding, construction and packing. If anything is substituted, list it and state how the bulk version will be approved. Do not write “approved” across unresolved differences.

Approval should identify the exact sample version and date, not merely the style name. The physical sample should be accompanied by the measurement chart, bill of materials, artwork, comments closed, packing instructions and quality criteria. Decide who keeps the buyer sample, factory sample and any third-party inspection reference.

10.Plan sample time and cost by decision gates

A simple logo trial on an available pouch can move faster than a new structured case with custom-dyed materials and hardware. Sample time includes clarification, sourcing, pattern work, decoration trials, sewing, internal review, courier and buyer response. One late approval can pause the next stage even when the factory has completed its work.

Ask for milestone dates rather than one total. Also separate sample cost from bulk unit cost. A prototype is often cut and sewn individually and may include sourcing or setup that becomes more efficient in production. 11. Connect the approved sample to bulk production

After approval, freeze the pattern, bill of materials, measurement chart, artwork, packaging and quality plan. The pre-production meeting should walk through the approved references and identify risks at cutting, decoration, zipper assembly, lining insertion, finishing and packing.

Review a first production piece or early line output before the whole order is complete. That step can reveal whether bulk material thickness, machinery or line handling changes the outcome. The broader Custom Bag Manufacturing Process shows how sampling hands off to material booking, cutting, production, inspection and delivery.

Common cosmetic bag sampling mistakes

  • Approving from photos only: photographs cannot prove hand feel, capacity, opening access or zipper behavior.

  • Using substitute contents: random bottles do not expose the constraints of the actual retail kit.

  • Changing several objectives at once: new size, material and logo direction make the cause of improvement or failure unclear.

  • Sending fragmented comments: several emails create conflicting instructions and untracked versions.

  • Approving a logo from a PDF: color, opacity, texture and puckering must be checked on the real substrate.

  • Ignoring the lining: an attractive exterior can hide loose lining, raw edges or inaccessible pockets.

  • Skipping pack-out: the bag may crush, crease or hide its logo inside the intended presentation.

  • Treating the sample as the entire QC standard: measurements, materials, tolerances and defect rules still need written controls.

  • Assuming repeat samples use unchanged inputs: reconfirm current material and component references before a repeat order.

The packaging interaction deserves its own review. Use Custom Cosmetic Bag Packaging for Beauty Brands when the bag is sold in a sleeve, box, beauty set or subscription pack.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a custom cosmetic bag sample take?

A straightforward first sample may take roughly 7–15 working days after the brief and available materials are confirmed. Custom color, special hardware, complex structure, several logo trials or missing decisions can extend the route.Add courier and buyer review time.

How much does a cosmetic bag sample cost?

The fee depends on pattern work, material sourcing, logo setup, tooling, complexity, number of versions and courier. A made-to-spec prototype is produced outside normal bulk efficiency, so its price should not be treated as the mass-production unit price.

Can I approve a cosmetic bag sample from photos?

Photos are useful for early screening, but physical review is safer for hand feel, color, capacity, opening access, zipper function, lining, structure and pack-out. If remote approval is unavoidable, agree on measured video checks, retained references and the remaining risk.

What is the difference between a prototype and a PP sample?

A prototype helps resolve design and function. A pre-production or PP sample should reflect the frozen production specification and intended inputs as closely as practical. Because terminology varies, define the required evidence for each sample in writing.

Should the sample use the final material?

Preferably when material behavior affects approval. A substitute can be useful for shape or construction if it is clearly identified. Do not use a substitute sample to approve final color, surface, thickness, seam bulk or logo performance.

How many sample rounds are normal?

There is no universal number. A clear adapted style may need one development sample plus a PP confirmation. A new structured design can require several focused rounds. The objective is not to minimize the count at any cost; it is to resolve the defined risks without repeatedly changing the brief.

Does approving a sample guarantee bulk quality?

No. The approved sample is one important reference. Bulk production still needs controlled materials, specifications, tolerances, first-piece review, in-line checks and final inspection.

What should I send with sample comments?

Identify the sample version and date, provide numbered annotated photos, state the observed condition and measurable required result, mark each point mandatory or optional, and request a written response and change log.

Approve a production reference, not just a nice prototype

A strong custom cosmetic bag sample development process moves from open questions to controlled evidence. The physical bag, material references, measurements, artwork, comments, packing and quality criteria must tell the same story before bulk work begins.
