Top 10 Non-Woven Shopping Bag Manufacturers in 2026

  • Product Introduction

Posted by Trimela On Sep 07 2026

Top 10 Non-Woven Shopping Bag Manufacturers in 2026A non-woven shopping bag looks simple, but its performance is decided long before the logo is printed. The polymer, fiber bonding, material weight, gusset, seam technology, handle reinforcement, print route and finished-bag test all affect whether the carrier survives real retail use. That is why a manufacturer shortlist should be based on the proposed construction—not on a generic claim that every bag is “reusable” or “eco-friendly.”

Ten companies worth investigating in 2026 are BAGFACTORY, BAG MAKERS, CPC Vietnam, Sapphire Packaging, Bright Vietnam, Vina Long Bag, Hariom PolyPacks, Sri Raja Bags, Rayson Nonwoven and Wenzhou Lianhai Bag. Their official sites describe relevant non-woven shopping-bag products and manufacturing activity. This guide identifies a useful buying angle for each company and the evidence to request before treating any website statement as applicable to your order.

How this list was prepared: Trimela reviewed first-party manufacturer and product pages on September 7, 2026. The numbers organize the comparison; they are not independently measured quality positions. We have not completed comparative factory audits, laboratory tests or purchase trials. Buyers should verify the legal seller, production site, process scope, certification status, material composition, capacity and commercial terms for the exact SKU and order.

1. BAGFACTORY: machine-made spunbond bags from Lithuania

BAGFACTORY states that it established its own manufacturing plant in Lithuania in 2015 and makes machine-produced bags from non-woven spunbond as well as paper. The company highlights a bonding process for reusable non-woven polypropylene bags and says it introduced recycled spunbond material into production in 2020.

This is a relevant European option for supermarket, department-store and promotional programmes built around repeatable machine-made formats. The most useful due-diligence question is how the bonding technology applies to the proposed bag: side seams, bottom formation, handle attachment and reinforcements can use different processes, and one successful seam does not establish the strength of the complete carrier.

Ask for the precise material code, polymer, GSM, layer structure and bonding settings or control limits relevant to the order. If recycled content or certification is required, request current scope and transaction evidence for the exact site and material. Do not generalize a company-level sustainability statement to every color, component or bag.

2. BAG MAKERS: a USA-made non-woven tote option

BAG MAKERS offers a USA-Made Non-Woven Patriot Tote and related formats. Its product page provides a defined stock construction, imprint area, packaging information and Union, Illinois FOB details. This makes the company useful to investigate for US programmes where a standard format and domestic production route may reduce overseas transit exposure.

The phrase “USA-made” still needs a process map. Ask where the spunbond fabric is produced, where it is cut and converted, where handles are attached, and where decoration occurs. Confirm whether the quoted item is the stock model, a modified version, or a fully custom bag following a different supply route.

For branded work, check the print method, maximum coverage, color count and tolerance on the chosen bag color. Stock availability and published production times are dynamic; require a dated written quotation tied to final artwork and quantity.

3. CPC Vietnam: multiple reusable-bag routes in southern Vietnam

CPC Vietnam describes factories in the Mekong Delta and a product range that includes non-woven PP, woven PP, RPET and other shopping bags. Its site also presents material handling, lamination, printing and assembly activities.

That breadth can help a retailer compare non-woven PP with an alternative material without starting a new supplier search. It also makes specification discipline essential. Woven PP, spunbond non-woven PP and RPET constructions have different surfaces, strength behavior, print routes and end-of-life considerations.

Ask CPC to identify the exact factory and line for each SKU, plus which operations are internal. Avoid relying on capacity totals, customer-logo galleries or certification badges without current supporting documents. A multi-factory group should provide change notification if production may transfer between sites.

4. Sapphire Packaging: PP non-woven and reusable formats from Vietnam

Sapphire Packaging describes an own-factory manufacturing route in Vietnam and lists PP non-woven shopping bags alongside woven PP, RPET, cooler and other reusable formats. The range is relevant to grocery and promotional buyers considering both unlaminated and printed/laminated constructions.

Before comparing prices, establish whether “non-woven” refers to PP spunbond or another material. For laminated bags, define the printed film, adhesive or thermal bond, base fabric and finish. A film-printed bag can deliver photographic graphics, but the extra layer changes stiffness, scuff behavior, fold recovery and recovery-route assumptions.

Ask for the current factory address, legal seller, fabric source and sample sequence. Any recycled-content statement needs a percentage boundary and order evidence; any certification needs the holder, site, scope, validity and transaction documents where applicable.

5. Bright Vietnam: a vertically coordinated non-woven route

Bright Vietnam states that it produces non-woven polypropylene fabric and manufactures shopping and promotional bags at a factory in Dong Nai Province. The company describes a coordinated route covering its own fabric, cutting, printing, sewing and finished products.

Vertical coordination can reduce ambiguity between material and bag conversion, especially when color consistency or material weight is critical. It does not eliminate the need for incoming and in-process controls. A manufacturer can produce its own spunbond and still purchase inks, film, thread, webbing or recycled feedstock from other sources.

Ask Bright to map every component and operation for the proposed bag. Confirm roll-level GSM tolerance, color reference, roll width, cutting orientation, seam type, thread specification and handle reinforcement.Where certificates appear on the website, obtain current documents and verify that their product and site scope apply to the order.

6. Vina Long Bag: Vietnamese factory address and broad bag assortment

Vina Long Bag identifies a factory in Hung Yen Province, Vietnam, and lists non-woven bags, PP non-woven bags, woven PP, RPET, cooler and other bag types. It can be investigated for buyers who need a straightforward non-woven carrier or a coordinated assortment across reusable constructions.

The published product range is a starting point, not proof that every process is performed at the stated factory. Ask whether fabric extrusion/spunbonding, film printing, lamination, cutting, sewing or ultrasonic bonding are internal or subcontracted. Name approved subcontract steps in the quality plan when they are critical.

Request a drawing and bill of materials with the sample. For a laminated box bag, confirm the film, base fabric, gusset, handle webbing, seam allowance and bottom reinforcement. For an unlaminated tote, focus on fabric uniformity, edge construction, print penetration and seam strength.

7. Hariom PolyPacks: Indian non-woven fabric and bag conversion

Hariom PolyPacks’ official non-woven bags page describes a factory in West Bengal and equipment for automatic bag making, flexographic, screen and offset printing. The company presents D-cut, U-cut, handle and box-bag formats.

This makes HPPL relevant to buyers comparing low-component punched-handle bags with more structured retail carriers.The production route should follow the intended service life. A light D-cut carrier for short use should not be evaluated by the same criteria as a gusseted box bag intended for repeated grocery trips.

Ask which line is compatible with the finished dimensions and GSM, and how handles or punched openings are reinforced. If the website’s recycled-material statement is relevant to the project, request the precise recycled percentage, feedstock basis and traceability evidence rather than repeating the general marketing wording.

8. Sri Raja Bags: regional Indian carry-bag manufacturing

Sri Raja Bags states that it manufactures non-woven fabric carry bags at Dhone in Andhra Pradesh. Its website also identifies several registered units trading under the Sri Raja Bags name.

The disclosure of operating names is useful, because it prompts an important procurement question: which entity will accept the purchase order, manufacture the bag, issue the invoice and stand behind a claim or test report? These should align, or their relationship should be documented.

For a regional retail programme, confirm available bag formats, artwork limits, production steps and delivery footprint. Do not infer export capability, certification or uniform capacity from domestic customer counts. If shipping outside India, add export packing, carton/pallet controls and destination documentation to the RFQ.

9. Rayson Nonwoven: spunbond production plus bag conversion in Foshan

Foshan Ruixin Non Woven Co., Ltd., marketed as Rayson, describes a manufacturing facility in Foshan, China, with spunbond non-woven production.Its company profile and non-woven carry-bag page present both fabric production and finished-bag conversion capability.

This route may suit a buyer who wants closer control of fabric color, width and GSM before the bag is cut. Ask whether the specific bag fabric will run on an internal line and how the roll is identified through cutting, printing and sewing. Material verticality is valuable only when traceability is maintained into the finished cartons.

Rayson’s product pages contain broad environmental, performance and certification wording. Treat those as company claims until the exact fabric/bag, standard, test report, holder, scope and validity are confirmed. Request a construction-specific load test rather than extrapolating from fabric tensile data.

10. Wenzhou Lianhai Bag: broad non-woven shopping-bag capability in China

Wenzhou Lianhai Bag Co., Ltd. states that it is located in Longgang, Wenzhou, and specializes in PET, PP and PE knitted and non-woven shopping bags along with other custom bag formats.

The range is relevant when a programme may need PP non-woven carriers, RPET options or related promotional products. Because the website spans several materials, insist on a separate specification and process route for each. Similar-looking bags can use different polymers, printing, coatings and seam methods.

Ask for the current producing site, equipment route, capacity allocated to the order and independent inspection access.Published daily output or “quick shipment” claims are dynamic marketing information; delivery should be defined from final approval and confirmed in writing for the exact quantity and season.

Printing routes for non-woven retail bags

The surface determines the artwork strategy. Unlaminated spunbond is porous and textured; a laminated bag behaves more like flexible packaging. Fine type and photographic gradients cannot be assumed to reproduce equally on both.

Screen printing on cut panels or finished bags

Screen printing can give strong spot-color graphics, but ink deposit, mesh, curing and fabric texture affect edge definition. Confirm maximum print area around seams and handles. Check blocking, odor, rub resistance and ink cracking after folding.

Flexographic printing on roll material

Flexo can support repeating larger runs before conversion. Artwork must account for plate, anilox, trapping and material stretch. A digital layout proof validates content; it does not demonstrate production color or registration.

Printed film plus lamination

Reverse-printing an OPP/BOPP film can protect graphics and deliver high coverage. The film, ink and bond become part of the bag.Approve gloss/matte appearance, color, lamination adhesion and crease performance. If recyclability is claimed, evaluate the complete structure in the destination context.

Environmental claims: avoid shortcuts with polypropylene

Non-woven PP is still plastic. Reuse may improve performance per use, but the outcome depends on bag mass, service life, return behavior, washing, transport and end-of-life infrastructure. “Non-woven,” “reusable” and “recyclable” are not equivalent statements.

If a recycled-content claim is proposed, define the percentage denominator and whether it covers the fabric or the entire bag. Obtain supplier and transaction documents appropriate to the programme. If a recyclability claim is proposed, assess the complete bag—lamination, ink, thread, handles, board, zipper and labels—and the actual collection route in the target market.

Avoid icons or claims that imply compostability or biodegradability unless the complete product has suitable evidence under an applicable standard and disposal condition. Read How to Avoid Greenwashing in Reusable Shopping Bag Marketing and How Material Weight Affects Shopping Bag Environmental Performance before finalizing packaging copy.

Frequently asked questions

What is a non-woven shopping bag made from?

Many retail non-woven bags use polypropylene spunbond, but the category can also include polyester/RPET or composites. The fibers or filaments are bonded rather than conventionally woven. Ask for the polymer, manufacturing process, GSM and every additional layer.

Is non-woven PP the same as woven PP?

No. Woven PP uses tapes interlaced into a fabric; spunbond non-woven PP bonds filaments into a sheet.Woven bags often suit heavier structured formats, while non-woven bags can be lighter and economical. Performance depends on the finished construction, not the category name.

What GSM is best for a reusable non-woven bag?

There is no universal best GSM. Bag size, polymer, fiber bonding, seam method, handle reinforcement, load, reuse target and humidity matter. A heavier sheet can still fail at a poorly designed handle. Specify performance, then validate the complete bag.

Are laminated non-woven bags stronger?

Lamination can add stiffness, print protection and moisture resistance, but it does not automatically solve seam or handle failures. The film-to-fabric bond, bag geometry and joining method must be tested. Lamination can also change recovery and recyclability considerations.

Is sewing better than ultrasonic bonding?

Not always. Sewing can suit structured gusseted bags and webbing handles; ultrasonic bonding can suit compatible automated constructions. The correct route is the one validated for the material, geometry, load and service target. Approve it as part of the specification.

Can a non-woven polypropylene bag be called eco-friendly?

That broad claim is risky. Use specific statements supported by evidence, such as a verified recycled-content percentage or a defined durability test. Consider material mass, actual reuse, transport and local end-of-life systems. Do not imply biodegradability or universal recyclability.

How many times should a reusable non-woven bag be used?

The answer depends on what it replaces and the impact method used.From a product perspective, define an intended number of load cycles and validate it with a finished-bag test. Our guide on how many times a reusable shopping bag must be used explains why one universal number is misleading.

What information produces an accurate custom quote?

Provide dimensions, polymer/material, GSM, color, lamination, joining, handle, artwork, print method, intended load/reuse, quantity by SKU, testing, evidence, packaging, destination and Incoterm. MOQ, price, sample cost, lead time, capacity and testing availability are order-specific and require written confirmation.
